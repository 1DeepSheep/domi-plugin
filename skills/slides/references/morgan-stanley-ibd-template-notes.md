# Morgan Stanley IBD Pitchbook Template Notes

Use this note when a deck needs Morgan Stanley investment banking / valuation-materials style, especially for IPO analysis, financing history, shareholder returns, valuation dispute, cap table, peer benchmarking, transaction premium, DCF, WACC, and scenario pages.

## Source Decks Reviewed

- `54-IBD-presentation-2020-Douyu-International-Holdings-Morgan-Stanley-1.pdf`
- `55-IBD-presentation-2019-Pivotal-Software-Inc-Morgan-Stanley-1.pdf`

The observations below refer to PDF page numbers. Borrow the composition and analytical grammar; do not copy branding, confidential markings, or bank-specific legal language.

## Global Visual Grammar

- Wide landscape pitchbook canvas with generous margins, white background, thin grey title rule, small footer/source line, and restrained blue/teal accents.
- Content is built around one primary exhibit, not around decorative blocks. A page feels full when the main exhibit is large, aligned, and interpretable, even if it has clean whitespace.
- Blue or teal highlights identify the selected case, target company, current offer, or key conclusion. Red is reserved for negative values, selected sensitivity ranges, warnings, or premium/discount emphasis.
- Dense tables use thin horizontal rules, light grey section bands, pale blue/green highlight rows, and bold subtotal lines. Avoid heavy borders unless the table is a qualitative disclosure grid.
- Text is compact and close to the evidence: numbered assumptions, short analytical bullets, side callouts, and source notes. Do not force a bottom four-card KPI strip when a side note, small chart, or inline annotation works better.
- Exhibits should have stable top and bottom alignment. In two-column pages, align chart/table tops and bottoms; source lines sit directly below their exhibit, not floating mid-page.

## Template Families To Reuse

### 1. Offer / Transaction Summary Table

Reference: Pivotal p3.

Use for offer terms, financing terms, acquisition terms, or IPO structure. Put the scenario / offer names in the left column, key economics in the middle, and implications on the right. Use colored row labels or bracket-like grouping only to clarify hierarchy.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: financing round summary, old-share transfer terms, lock-up categories, or key HKEX terms.

### 2. Football Field Valuation Summary

References: Douyu p9-p11; Pivotal p17.

Use when comparing valuation methodologies. Left side lists numbered methodologies and assumptions; center/right shows valuation ranges as horizontal bars or vertical range markers; a small side table gives selected implied values and deltas.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: compare latest pre-IPO valuation, comparable company PS/ARR multiples, DCF range, overseas peer range, and downside public-market reference.

### 3. Valuation / Sensitivity Matrix

References: Douyu p8; Pivotal p18.

Use when the conclusion depends on multiple assumptions. Build a full-width grid with method / case rows and valuation outputs by assumption columns. Highlight the base case and the selected column/row with pale shading, brackets, or a thin outline.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: PS multiple vs revenue scenario, GM recovery vs cost ratio, user conversion vs pricing, or IPO price vs pre-IPO investor returns.

### 4. Share Price Or Multiple Trend With Event Callouts

References: Douyu p12-p13; Pivotal p4 and p6.

Use for stock price history, valuation multiple history, revenue multiple de-rating, or sector benchmark movement. Main chart spans most of the page; events are labeled directly on the chart with callout boxes. Short text above or beside the chart states why the period matters.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: external comparable valuation changes, cloud/MaaS peer multiple trend, or public competitor drawdown around pricing/AI capex news.

### 5. Scenario Comparison Small Multiples

References: Pivotal p9-p10 and p13.

Use for bull/base/bear or management case comparisons. Use 2x2 or 2x4 small charts with identical axes and clear colored scenario labels. Each panel answers the same metric question.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: revenue build by case, GM recovery path, cash runway scenarios, customer concentration by case, or token demand/pricing sensitivity.

### 6. Peer Benchmarking Grid

References: Pivotal p19-p21 and p28.

Use when comparing peers on operating metrics or valuation. Options include 2x2 grouped bar charts, ranked bar charts, and scatter plots with labeled points. Highlight the subject company with a different color and add median/range markers.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: PS vs growth, ARR/revenue vs valuation, GM vs revenue scale, customer concentration vs peers, or market share ranking.

### 7. Precedent Transaction / Premium Analysis

References: Douyu p17; Pivotal p29-p30.

Use for transaction premium, precedent deal multiples, and takeover benchmark pages. Horizontal bar charts work well when there are many deals. Include median, selected target, and outlier annotations.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: compare pre-IPO round valuation uplift, old-share transfer valuation, strategic investment premium, or lock-up pressure by shareholder class.

### 8. DCF / Financial Model Page

References: Douyu p22-p23 and p27; Pivotal p22-p26.

Use for dense model output. Left side or top band states core assumptions; main table shows forecast metrics; lower-right or side panel shows sensitivity matrix. Keep the model readable with white horizontal-rule tables and bold subtotals.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: public-cloud UE model, revenue-to-gross-profit bridge, adjusted loss to OCF, or valuation sensitivity.

### 9. WACC / Assumption Build-Up

References: Douyu p24-p25 and p28; Pivotal p33.

Use for assumption-heavy pages. Put formula inputs and ranges in a compact table, highlight the selected case, and use a small chart or sensitivity table to show impact. Red dashed highlighting can mark the active assumption column, but use it sparingly.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: discount rate, terminal multiple, public-market peer multiple, GM recovery assumption, or required public-cloud cost reduction.

### 10. Contribution / Exchange Ratio / Ownership Table

References: Douyu p30-p31.

Use when the core question is ownership, contribution, or dilution. Use a full-width table with scenario columns and contribution metrics; small side tables can show cap table or pro forma ownership. Highlight rows that determine negotiation leverage.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: shareholder structure, lock-up schedule, H-share full circulation, largest external investor, old-share proceeds, or post-IPO sell-down pressure.

### 11. Corporate Structure / Entity Map

Reference: Pivotal p35.

Use for VIE, red-chip, founder vehicle, employee platform, or shareholder entity explanations. Use boxes and arrows only when they simplify legal structure; keep labels short and footnote the ownership source.

Adaptation for IPO analysis: founder/employee platforms, CVC vehicles, investment funds, and related-party entities.

## How To Choose A Template

- If the page asks “what is the valuation worth?” use football field or valuation matrix.
- If it asks “which investor made money and why?” use return decomposition table plus two targeted charts, not four KPI cards.
- If it asks “does the business model work?” use financial model / bridge page with left data and right implication.
- If it asks “how does this company compare with peers?” use benchmarking grid, ranking bar, or scatter.
- If it asks “what changed over time?” use trend chart with event callouts.
- If it asks “who owns what / who can sell?” use contribution / ownership / cap table layout.
- If it asks “what terms matter?” use offer / transaction summary table.

## Adaptation Rules For Investment-Analysis Decks

- Keep `IPO FILE ANALYSIS` / user brand labels from the current deck; do not imitate Morgan Stanley / MUFG logos.
- Use the existing Morgan Stanley style lock unless the user explicitly changes page size. These IBD decks use custom wide pages; borrow their layout logic, not necessarily their exact canvas.
- Use Chinese content where the report is Chinese. English subtitles are useful for compact financial table headings or chart titles, but should not replace the analysis language.
- For financial and operating data, use Morgan Stanley white horizontal-rule table style. For qualitative disclosure, sources, risks, and long transaction descriptions, use black-header information tables.
- Page titles must be thesis titles with numbers or sharp conclusions. Avoid directory titles such as “融资历史” when the page can say “七轮融资不只是增资，三次老股交易已让创始人套现”.
- Avoid mandatory metric strips. Use bottom cards only if each card adds a distinct conclusion. Prefer side callouts, inline labels, small sensitivity tables, or a compact proof strip.
- When multiple charts are on one page, align their top and bottom edges, equalize exhibit heights, and enlarge the main evidence until axes and labels are legible.
- Source notes align to the left edge of the exhibit they support. If there are two exhibits, each can have its own source line; if there is one page-level source, align it to the main content grid.

## QA Checklist

- Does the page have one primary analytical job and one dominant evidence structure?
- Did the selected template match the analytical job, or was it chosen only to fill space?
- Is the main exhibit large enough to read when printed?
- Are paired charts/tables aligned by top edge, bottom edge, and source position?
- Are selected cases and key rows highlighted sparingly?
- Is whitespace supporting readability rather than exposing missing analysis?
- Are there any repeated four-card strips that could be replaced by a side note, chart, table, or direct label?
- Does every chart/table include unit, source, and key calculation caveat?
